Depletion PMOS (D-PMOS) Transistor Physics

0. Historical Origin: Ion-Implanted Buried Channels & Dual-Mode Loads (1960s–1970s)

Step 0 · Origins · Bell Labs & Silicon-Gate Era

Following the invention of the insulated-gate MOSFET by Mohamed Atalla and Dawon Kahng (1959), designers needed a single-polarity transistor that conducted at zero gate bias to act as a compact, linear-behaving active load resistor. In the late 1960s and 1970s, ion-implantation pioneer advances allowed engineers to introduce shallow dopant profiles directly beneath the gate oxide, shifting threshold voltages across zero without corrupting oxide integrity.

From Native Inversion to Precision Implant

Early P-channel metal-gate processes struggled with positive fixed oxide charge that pulled enhancement thresholds deeply negative, whereas controlled boron/BF₂ channel implants enabled tunable buried P-channels in N-well tubs for analog current sources, switched-capacitor bias chains, and dual-mode PMOS loads prior to full CMOS standardization.

Why Simulate D-PMOS Today?

Understanding how a positive gate bias depletes a pre-formed hole channel until full pinch-off (VGS ≥ +0.80V) completes the symmetric enhancement/depletion MOSFET design space alongside D-NMOS.

1. Device Architecture, Doping & Normally-ON Conduction

Implanted P-Layer in N-Well, IDSS = 0.10 mA, VTH = +0.80V

The Depletion P-Channel MOSFET (D-PMOS) is fabricated inside an N-well tub embedded within a P-substrate wafer. A shallow acceptor-doped P-type layer (boron or BF₂ dose ΦA ≈ 1×10¹² cm⁻²) is pre-implanted directly under the gate oxide between the heavily doped P+ Source and Drain regions.

1. Built-in P-Channel

At zero gate bias (VGS = 0V), the metallurgical P-channel is populated with free hole carriers. A robust zero-bias current (IDSS ≈ 0.102 mA) flows when VSD is applied.

2. Positive Threshold (VTH > 0)

In D-PMOS, gate-to-source threshold voltage is positive: VTH = +0.80V. A positive gate bias is required to deplete holes and turn the transistor OFF.

3. Normally-ON Property

Current flows effortlessly at zero gate bias without needing inversion. Applying gate voltage modulates the channel cross-sectional thickness above or below nominal.

2. Dual-Mode Operation: Depletion Mode vs. Enhancement Mode

Depletion (VGS > 0) vs. Enhancement (VGS < 0)

Because the channel is pre-formed, D-PMOS supports dual-mode gate control:

Cutoff / Full Pinch-Off (VGS ≥ VTH)

When positive gate bias VGS ≥ +0.80V is applied, the gate depletion region expands downward across the entire P-channel thickness, repelling all mobile holes: ISD = 0.

Depletion Mode (0V < VGS < VTH)

Positive gate voltage pushes holes into the N-well, narrowing the effective channel depth. Hole current is reduced below the zero-bias level IDSS.

Enhancement Mode (VGS < 0V)

Negative gate voltage attracts additional holes to the surface, accumulating carriers on top of the implanted channel. This boosts ISD well above IDSS.

3. The Pinch-Off Mechanism in Depletion PMOS

VSD,sat = VTH - VGS = 0.80V - VGS

Source is tied to positive potential VDD while Drain is at a lower voltage (VSD = VS - VD ≥ 0). Along the channel from Source to Drain, local gate-to-channel reverse bias increases.

Pinch-Off Boundary Equation: VSD = VSD,sat = VTH - VGS  (for VGS < VTH)
With VTH = +0.80 V, pinch-off at zero gate bias (VGS = 0V) occurs at exactly VSD,sat = 0.80V - 0V = +0.80 V!

Why and How Pinch-Off Occurs:

Linear Regime (VSD < VSD,sat)

The implanted hole channel is continuous between Source and Drain. The channel cross-section is deeper at the Source and tapers toward the Drain.

Beyond Pinch-Off (VSD ≥ VSD,sat)

The depletion region merges at the Drain edge. The channel pinches off, and excess voltage VSD - VSD,sat drops across the drain space-charge layer.

Carrier Transport Beyond Pinch-Off: Mobile holes traveling down the channel reach the pinch-off point and are swept across the drain space-charge layer by the intense lateral electric field into the Drain at saturation velocity. Hole current ISD reaches a saturation plateau.

4. Operating Regions, Analytical Formulations & Shockley Form

kp = 0.32 mA/V², VTH = +0.80V, IDSS = 0.102 mA

Define the PMOS process transconductance parameter as kp = μp · Cox · (Wp / L) = 0.32 mA/V². Overdrive voltage is defined as VOV = VTH - VGS = 0.80V - VGS.

6. Analog Precision & Power IC Applications

High-Side Current Sources, Voltage Level Shifters
High-Side Constant Current Source

With gate tied to source (VGS = 0V), D-PMOS acts as a high-side current source connected to VDD, supplying constant current IDSS down to low drain voltages.

Zero-Headroom Level Shifters

Because D-PMOS requires no gate threshold voltage drop to turn ON, it is used in analog level shifters and low-dropout regulators requiring minimal voltage headroom.
