Enhancement NMOS (E-NMOS) Transistor Physics

Band bending, electrostatic surface inversion, pinch-off boundary mechanics, and complete current-voltage formulations.

P-Substrate · N+ S/D · Normally OFF (VTH > 0)
Jump to: 0. Origin Story 1. Structure & Doping 2. Inversion & Threshold 3. Pinch-Off Mechanism 4. Operating Regions & Equations 5. Live Graph & Locus Sync 6. Second-Order Effects

0. The Origin Story: Atalla, Kahng & the Birth of the MOSFET

Bell Labs, 1959 & Fairchild, 1963

Julius Lilienfeld patented the concept of an insulated-gate field-effect transistor in 1925, yet for three decades every prototype failed because dangling silicon surface bonds trapped charge before an electric field could invert the channel.

1. Thermal SiO₂ Passivation (1959)

At Bell Labs, Mohamed Atalla discovered that thermally growing ultra-pure silicon dioxide (SiO₂) chemically passivated surface states, unlocking clean electrostatic field control.

2. First Working MOSFET (Nov 1959)

Dawon Kahng and Atalla fabricated the first working metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor—laying the physical blueprint for every planar NMOS and PMOS switch.

3. Complementary Logic (1963)

Frank Wanlass at Fairchild Semiconductor paired enhancement N-channel and P-channel MOSFETs into complementary logic, launching modern ultra-dense VLSI scaling.

1. Device Architecture, Doping & Normally-OFF Mode

P-Sub, N+ S/D, LDD, Poly Gate

The Enhancement N-Channel MOSFET (E-NMOS) is a four-terminal field-effect transistor fabricated on a moderately doped single-crystal P-type silicon substrate (boron acceptor concentration NA ≈ 1×10¹⁵ cm⁻³). Source and Drain contacts consist of heavily doped N-type wells (arsenic or phosphorus donor concentration ND ≈ 1×10²⁰ cm⁻³) separated by a channel of physical metallurgical length L and width W.

1. Substrate & Bulk (B)

P-type bulk provides the crystal lattice and forms back-to-back PN junctions with source and drain. Tied to GND (0V) to guarantee reverse-bias isolation.

2. LDD / SDE Extensions

Lightly Doped Drain extensions under the sidewall spacers reduce peak lateral electric fields near the drain edge, suppressing hot-carrier degradation.

3. Normally-OFF Conduction

At zero gate bias (VGS = 0V), the back-to-back PN junctions prevent electron transit between source and drain. Drain current is virtually zero (cutoff).

Because no conductive channel exists at thermal equilibrium without an externally applied electric field, the enhancement NMOS is intrinsically normally OFF. Conduction requires positive gate bias above the threshold voltage.

2. Surface Inversion & Threshold Voltage (VTH > 0)

ψs = 2φF, Dynamic VTH(L, tox, VDS)

When a positive voltage VGS is applied to the polysilicon/metal gate relative to the substrate, positive electric field lines terminate on negative charges in the semiconductor, driving three sequential physical regimes:

Accumulation (VGS < 0V)

Negative gate voltage attracts majority holes to the Si/SiO₂ interface, reinforcing the P-type surface and preventing conduction.

Depletion (0V < VGS < VTH)

Positive gate voltage repels mobile holes, leaving uncompensated ionized acceptor atoms (NA⁻) that form an insulating space-charge depletion layer of depth Wdep.

Strong Inversion (VGS ≥ VTH)

Band bending pulls the conduction band below the Fermi level. Free minority electrons accumulate at the oxide interface, creating a conductive N-type channel.

VTH(L, tox, VDS) = VTH0(tox) - ΔVTH,SCE(L) - η(L)·VDS
Long-channel baseline: VTH0 ≈ 0.45 V (at tox = 2.0 nm)  ·  Oxide Capacitance: Cox = εox / tox

The effective gate overdrive voltage driving carrier inversion is defined as VOV = VGS - VTH. When VGS > VTH, the electron sheet charge density is given by Qinv(y) = -Cox [VGS - VTH - V(y)].

3. Pinch-Off & Velocity Saturation Boundary

VDsat = VOV·(Esat·L) / (VOV + Esat·L)

A foundational concept in MOSFET operation is channel pinch-off. As drain-to-source bias VDS is applied, electron drift current flows from Source to Drain, increasing the local channel potential from source to drain. In scaled devices, high lateral electric fields also induce carrier velocity saturation before classical pinch-off is reached.

VDsat = [VOV · (Esat · L)] / (VOV + Esat · L)   (Long-channel limit: VDsat → VOV)
At VDS = VDsat, carriers reach saturation velocity vsat and the channel pinches off near the drain, leaving depletion length ΔL = L - Leff.

Why and How Pinch-Off Occurs:

Linear / Triode (VDS < VDsat)

The inversion channel remains continuous and unbroken from source to drain. The channel profile is tapered (deeper at source, shallower at drain), behaving as a voltage-controlled resistor with high-field mobility reduction.

Beyond Pinch-Off (VDS ≥ VDsat)

The inversion channel pinches off near the drain. Increasing VDS shifts the pinch-off point toward the source, widening the high-field depletion gap ΔL and reducing effective channel length Leff.

Carrier Transport Beyond Pinch-Off: Even though the inversion layer depth pinches down at the drain end, current continues flowing! Electrons traveling down the channel reach the pinch-off point and are swept across the high-field drain depletion space-charge layer at saturation velocity (vsat ≈ 1×10⁷ cm/s).

4. Operating Regions & Complete Governing Formulations

kn = μn·Cox·(W/L), λ ∝ 1/L

Define the NMOS device transconductance parameter as kn = μn · Cox · (W / L), where Cox = 3.9ε0 / tox. Scaling W, L, and tox in the Device Geometry sandbox directly modulates kn, VTH, VDsat, and λ:

6. Second-Order & Short-Channel Physical Effects

VTH Roll-off, DIBL, Vel-Sat, CLM
VTH Roll-off (Charge Sharing)

Below L ≈ 50 nm, source and drain depletion regions share bulk charge beneath the gate, lowering threshold voltage exponentially:

ΔVTH,SCE ∝ exp(-L / Lc)  (Lc ≈ 25 nm)
Drain-Induced Barrier Lowering (DIBL)

In short-channel transistors, high drain bias penetrates into the channel, lowering the source-channel barrier (~0.08 V/V at 28 nm, near 0 at 180 nm):

ΔVTH,DIBL = -η(L) · VDS
Channel-Length Modulation (λ ∝ 1/L)

In saturation, depletion width ΔL grows with √(VDS - VDsat), increasing output slope λ ∝ 1/L much more strongly at short gate lengths than long channels.

Body Effect (Substrate Sensitivity)

When source-to-body reverse bias VSB > 0 is applied, the depletion layer widens, increasing threshold voltage via γ = √(2q·εSi·NA) / Cox.

Subthreshold Swing: S = [∂(log₁₀ IDS) / ∂VGS]⁻¹ = ln(10) · (kT/q) · [1 + Cdep/Cox] ≈ 70–85 mV/decade
S represents the gate voltage swing required to change subthreshold leakage current by one order of magnitude at room temperature (300K).
