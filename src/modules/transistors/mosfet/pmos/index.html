0. The Origin Story: From Atalla & Kahng to Complementary P-Channel Logic

Bell Labs, 1959 & Fairchild, 1963

Following Mohamed Atalla and Dawon Kahng's 1959 MOSFET breakthrough at Bell Labs, early commercial MOS integrated circuits actually favored P-channel (PMOS) transistors because positive sodium contamination in 1960s gate oxides shifted NMOS devices into erratic depletion behavior while keeping PMOS reliably normally-OFF.

1. SiO₂ Surface Control (1959)

Atalla and Kahng proved thermally grown SiO₂ could passivate silicon traps well enough to invert an N-type region into a conducting hole channel under negative gate bias.

2. Early PMOS Dominance (1960s)

PMOS enabled the first commercial MOS shift registers, calculators, and memories—despite lower hole mobility—before ultra-clean gate oxidation tamed NMOS.

3. Wanlass's CMOS Pairing (1963)

Frank Wanlass at Fairchild embedded PMOS alongside NMOS via complementary N-well/P-substrate engineering, making PMOS the essential rail-to-rail pull-up switch in all modern CMOS.

1. Device Architecture, N-Well Tub & Normally-OFF Mode

N-Well Tub, P+ S/D, Hole Conduction

The Enhancement P-Channel MOSFET (E-PMOS) is fabricated inside an engineered N-well tub (donor concentration ND ≈ 1×10¹⁶ cm⁻³) embedded within the standard P-type silicon wafer. Heavily doped P-type source and drain regions (boron concentration NA ≈ 1×10²⁰ cm⁻³) flank the channel.

1. N-Well Tub Isolation

The N-well is biased to the most positive power rail (VDD = +1.0V to +1.2V) via an N+ well tap to keep the well-to-substrate PN junction reverse-biased and isolate PMOS from neighboring circuits.

2. Hole Drift Conduction

Conduction is carried by positively charged holes (h+). Due to higher effective mass in the valence band, hole mobility (μp ≈ 220 cm²/(V·s)) is roughly 2.5× lower than electron mobility.

3. Channel Width Sizing (Wp ≈ 2Wn)

To match the drive current and switching speed of an NMOS device of identical gate length, PMOS channel width must be sized wider: Wp ≈ (2 to 3) × Wn.

At zero gate-to-source bias (VGS = 0V or VSG = 0V), the back-to-back P+/N-well junctions completely block current transit. Thus, E-PMOS is inherently normally OFF.

2. Surface Inversion & Negative Threshold Voltage (VTH < 0)

VTH,p = -0.80V, |VTH,p| = +0.80V

To induce a conductive channel in PMOS, a negative voltage must be applied to the gate with respect to the source and N-well. In circuit design, it is standard practice to express voltages using positive source-referenced notations:

VSG = VS - VG = -VGS  >  |VTH,p|  ≈  +0.80 V
Negative threshold: VTH,p ≈ -0.80 V  ·  Effective Overdrive: VOV = VSG - |VTH,p| = |VGS| - |VTH,p|

Physical Inversion Process: When VSG > |VTH,p| (or VGS < VTH,p), the negative gate potential bends energy bands upward, repelling majority electrons from the N-well surface and creating an initial depletion layer. Once the surface potential reaches strong inversion (ψs = -2φFn), minority holes from the P+ source and drain are attracted to the interface, forming a continuous conductive P-type inversion layer.

3. The Pinch-Off Mechanism in P-Channel Transistors

VSD,sat = VSG - |VTH,p|, VDG = |VTH,p|

In PMOS operation, the Source is typically held at positive supply VDD while the Drain is at a lower voltage (VSD = VS - VD ≥ 0). Along the channel from Source (y=0, V=0 relative to source) to Drain (y=L, V=VSD), the local source-to-channel potential increases, which reduces the local gate oxide potential.

Pinch-Off Boundary Condition: VSD = VSD,sat = VSG - |VTH,p|  ⟺  VDG = |VTH,p|
In standard VGS/VDS coordinates: |VDS| = |VDS,sat| = |VGS - VTH,p|  ⟺  VGD = VTH,p

Why and How Pinch-Off Occurs:

Linear Regime (VSD < VSD,sat)

The hole inversion layer extends continuously from Source to Drain. The channel depth is deepest at the Source and tapers toward the Drain as local overdrive shrinks.

Beyond Pinch-Off (VSD ≥ VSD,sat)

The hole inversion layer thickness drops to zero at the Drain edge. The pinch-off boundary shifts slightly toward the source, creating a high-field space charge region ΔL.

Hole Transport Beyond Pinch-Off: When holes drifting down the channel reach the pinch-off point, they encounter an intense lateral electric field directed from Source to Drain. This field sweeps the holes across the depletion region into the Drain at saturation velocity. The hole current ISD reaches saturation and remains nearly constant regardless of further increase in VSD.

4. Operating Regions & Complete Governing Formulations

kp = 0.32 mA/V², λ = 0.08 V⁻¹

Define the PMOS process transconductance parameter as kp = μp · Cox · (Wp / L) (nominal model value: kp = 0.32 mA/V²). The operating regions are mathematically partitioned as follows:

Operating Region Source-Referenced Conditions Analytical Current Equation (ISD) Channel State
Cutoff VSG ≤ |VTH,p|
(VGS ≥ VTH,p)		 ISD = 0  (Ideal drift)
Subthreshold leakage only		 No channel; P+/N-well barrier blocks holes
Linear (Triode) VSG > |VTH,p|
VSD < VSG - |VTH,p|		 ISD = kp · [(VSG - |VTH,p|)·VSD - ½·VSD²] Continuous resistive inverted P-channel
Saturation VSG > |VTH,p|
VSD ≥ VSG - |VTH,p|		 ISD = ½·kp·(VSG - |VTH,p|)² · (1 + λ·VSD) Pinched off at drain; velocity-saturated hole sweep
Small-Signal Transconductance: gm = ∂ISD/∂VSG = kp · (VSG - |VTH,p|) = √(2 · kp · ISD)
Output Resistance: ro = ∂VSD/∂ISD ≈ 1 / (λ · ISD,sat)  ·  Intrinsic Gain: Av0 = gm · ro = 2 / [λ · (VSG - |VTH,p|)]

6. Second-Order Effects, Body Bias & Latchup Prevention

Body Effect, Substrate Sensitivity, Latchup
PMOS Body Effect

If the N-well body is biased higher than the Source (VBS = VWell - VS > 0), the threshold magnitude increases:

|VTH,p| = |VTH0,p| + γp · [√(2φFn + VBS) - √(2φFn)]
CMOS Latchup Prevention

Parasitic bipolar transistors (vertical PNP and lateral NPN) form a silicon-controlled rectifier (SCR). Frequent N+ well taps to VDD and P+ substrate taps to GND clamp substrate resistance, suppressing latchup.
