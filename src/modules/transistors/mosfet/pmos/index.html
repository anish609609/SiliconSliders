0. The Origin Story: From Atalla & Kahng to Complementary P-Channel Logic

Bell Labs, 1959 & Fairchild, 1963

Following Mohamed Atalla and Dawon Kahng's 1959 MOSFET breakthrough at Bell Labs, early commercial MOS integrated circuits actually favored P-channel (PMOS) transistors because positive sodium contamination in 1960s gate oxides shifted NMOS devices into erratic depletion behavior while keeping PMOS reliably normally-OFF.

1. SiO₂ Surface Control (1959)

Atalla and Kahng proved thermally grown SiO₂ could passivate silicon traps well enough to invert an N-type region into a conducting hole channel under negative gate bias.

2. Early PMOS Dominance (1960s)

PMOS enabled the first commercial MOS shift registers, calculators, and memories—despite lower hole mobility—before ultra-clean gate oxidation tamed NMOS.

3. Wanlass's CMOS Pairing (1963)

Frank Wanlass at Fairchild embedded PMOS alongside NMOS via complementary N-well/P-substrate engineering, making PMOS the essential rail-to-rail pull-up switch in all modern CMOS.

1. Device Architecture, N-Well Tub & Normally-OFF Mode

N-Well Tub, P+ S/D, Hole Conduction

The Enhancement P-Channel MOSFET (E-PMOS) is fabricated inside an engineered N-well tub (donor concentration ND ≈ 1×10¹⁶ cm⁻³) embedded within the standard P-type silicon wafer. Heavily doped P-type source and drain regions (boron concentration NA ≈ 1×10²⁰ cm⁻³) flank the channel.

1. N-Well Tub Isolation

The N-well is biased to the most positive power rail (VDD = +1.0V to +1.2V) via an N+ well tap to keep the well-to-substrate PN junction reverse-biased and isolate PMOS from neighboring circuits.

2. Hole Drift Conduction

Conduction is carried by positively charged holes (h+). Lower effective hole mobility (μp ≈ 0.45 μn) yields proportionally lower drive current than E-NMOS at identical W/L.

3. Channel Width Sizing (Wp ≈ 2Wn)

To match the drive current and switching speed of an NMOS device of identical gate length, PMOS channel width must be sized wider: Wp ≈ (2 to 3) × Wn.

At zero gate-to-source bias (VGS = 0V or VSG = 0V), the back-to-back P+/N-well junctions completely block current transit. Thus, E-PMOS is inherently normally OFF.

2. Surface Inversion & Dynamic Negative Threshold Voltage (VTH < 0)

Long-Ch VTH0,p ≈ -0.45 V (SCE shifts |VTH| lower)

To induce a conductive hole channel in PMOS, a negative voltage must be applied to the gate with respect to the source and N-well (equivalently VSG = -VGS > |VTH,p|):

VTH,p = −max(0.10, |VTH0,p(tox)| − ΔVTH,SCE(L) − η(L)·VSD)
Long-Channel VTH0,p ≈ −0.45 V at tox = 2.0 nm  ·  Overdrive: VOV = VSG − |VTH,p|

Physical Inversion & Oxide Scaling: Thinner gate oxide tox raises oxide capacitance Cox = 3.9ε0 / tox, strengthening gate control over the N-well surface. Below L ≈ 50 nm, short-channel charge sharing and DIBL reduce |VTH,p| dynamically.

3. Pinch-Off & Velocity Saturation in P-Channel Transistors

VSD,sat = VOV·(EsatL) / (VOV + EsatL)

In PMOS operation, Source is held at positive potential while Drain is pulled lower (VSD = -VDS ≥ 0). In short channels, lateral fields saturate hole drift velocity early, clamping VSD,sat well below VOV:

VSD,sat = VOV · (EsatL) / (VOV + EsatL)  |  ΔL(VSD > VSD,sat) grows toward Source
Long-channel limit (EsatL ≫ VOV): VSD,sat → VOV  ·  Short-channel limit: current scales more linearly with VOV
Linear Regime (VSD < VSD,sat)

The hole inversion layer extends continuously from Source to Drain, tapering from Source toward Drain as local gate overdrive decreases.

Saturation Beyond Pinch-Off (VSD ≥ VSD,sat)

Hole channel thickness drops to zero at Leff = L − ΔL; high-field transport sweeps holes rapidly across ΔL into the P+ Drain.

4. Geometry-Scaled Governing Formulations

kp = μp·Cox·(W/L), λ ∝ 1/L

Define the PMOS transconductance parameter as kp = μp · Cox · (W / L) with μp ≈ 0.45 μn and Cox = 3.9ε0 / tox. Scaling L, W, and tox in Device Geometry updates every parameter dynamically:

Cutoff VSG ≤ |VTH,p|
(VGS ≥ VTH,p)		 ISD = 0  (Ideal drift)
Subthreshold: Isub ∝ exp[q(VSG-|VTH,p|)/nkT]		 No channel; P+/N-well barrier blocks holes
Linear (Triode) VSG > |VTH,p|
VSD < VSD,sat		 ISD = [kp / (1 + VSD/(EsatL))] · [VOV·VSD - ½·VSD²] Continuous resistive inverted P-channel
Saturation VSG > |VTH,p|

Output Resistance: ro ≈ 1 / (λ · ISD,sat)  |  Effective Length: Leff = max(0.2L, L − ΔL)
At matched geometry (W, L, tox), E-PMOS delivers ~45% of E-NMOS drive current due to μp ≈ 0.45 μn.

6. Short-Channel Effects, Body Bias & Latchup Prevention

|VTH| Roll-off, DIBL, Body Effect, Latchup
|VTH,p| Roll-off & DIBL

Below 50 nm, P+ source/drain charge sharing (exp(-L / Lc)) and drain field penetration (η(L)·VSD) reduce |VTH,p| dynamically, while λ ∝ 1/L increases saturation slope.

PMOS Body Effect & Latchup Prevention

N-well taps tied cleanly to VDD suppress latchup SCR paths and stabilize |VTH,p| against reverse well-to-source bias VBS.
