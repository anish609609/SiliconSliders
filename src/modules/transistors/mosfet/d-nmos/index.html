Depletion NMOS (D-NMOS) Transistor Physics

Pre-implanted metallurgical N-channel, normally ON zero-bias conduction, dual-mode operation, and Shockley equations.

Implanted N-Channel · Normally ON (VTH < 0) · Dual Mode

1. Device Architecture, Doping & Normally-ON Conduction

Implanted N-Layer, IDSS = 0.21 mA, VTH = -0.80V

The Depletion N-Channel MOSFET (D-NMOS) features a shallow donor-doped N-channel layer intentionally pre-implanted beneath the gate oxide into the P-type silicon substrate (donor implant dose ΦD ≈ 1×10¹² cm⁻²). This metallurgical layer provides a permanent conductive highway connecting the heavily doped N+ Source and Drain regions even with zero gate-to-source bias (VGS = 0V).

1. Built-in Channel Highway

At zero external gate bias, high free electron density already populates the implanted channel. Substantial current (IDSS ≈ 0.208 mA) flows when VDS is applied.

2. Negative Threshold (VTH < 0)

Because a conductive channel pre-exists, the gate threshold voltage is negative (VTH = VP ≈ -0.80V), representing the pinch-off voltage required to deplete the channel.

3. Normally-ON Property

Unlike enhancement MOSFETs which start in cutoff at zero bias, depletion MOSFETs are inherently conductive and require negative bias to turn OFF.

2. Dual-Mode Operation: Depletion Mode vs. Enhancement Mode

Depletion (VGS < 0) vs. Enhancement (VGS > 0)

D-NMOS is uniquely versatile because it operates in two distinct physical gate regimes:

Cutoff / Full Pinch-Off (VGS ≤ VTH)

When VGS ≤ -0.80V, the gate depletion region expands downward across the entire implanted channel thickness, completely cutting off electron transit: ID = 0.

Depletion Mode (VTH < VGS < 0V)

Negative gate voltage repels electrons toward the substrate, narrowing the effective cross-sectional channel depth. Drain current is reduced below the zero-bias level IDSS.

Enhancement Mode (VGS > 0V)

Positive gate voltage attracts additional electrons to the oxide interface, forming a surface accumulation layer on top of the implanted channel. This boosts ID well above IDSS.

This dual-mode flexibility allows D-NMOS to operate either as an electronically controlled variable resistor or as a robust constant current source.

3. The Pinch-Off Mechanism & Boundary Conditions

VDS,sat = VGS - VTH = VGS - (-0.80V) = VGS + 0.80V

In a depletion MOSFET, channel pinch-off is governed by the simultaneous action of gate depletion from the top surface and substrate junction depletion from the bottom.

Pinch-Off Boundary Equation: VDS = VDS,sat = VGS - VTH = VGS + |VTH|  ⟺  VGD = VTH
With VTH = -0.80 V, pinch-off at zero gate bias (VGS = 0V) occurs at exactly VDS,sat = 0 - (-0.80V) = +0.80 V!

Why and How Pinch-Off Occurs:

Linear / Triode (VDS < VDS,sat)

The implanted channel remains open throughout its entire length from Source to Drain. The channel depth narrows toward the Drain due to increasing local reverse bias VGD(y).

Beyond Pinch-Off (VDS ≥ VDS,sat)

The gate depletion region merges with the substrate depletion region at the Drain boundary. The channel pinches off, and excess voltage VDS - VDS,sat drops across the high-field drain depletion gap.

Carrier Transport Beyond Pinch-Off: Electrons traversing the open channel arrive at the pinch-off point and are rapidly propelled across the drain depletion region by the intense lateral electric field (Ex > 10⁴ V/cm) at saturation velocity (vsat ≈ 1×10⁷ cm/s). Drain current saturates at a constant plateau with modest slope due to channel-length modulation.

4. Operating Regions, Analytical Formulations & Shockley Form

kn = 0.65 mA/V², VTH = -0.80V, IDSS = 0.208 mA

Define the process transconductance parameter as kn = μn · Cox · (W / L) = 0.65 mA/V². Overdrive voltage is defined as VOV = VGS - VTH = VGS - (-0.80V) = VGS + 0.80V.

6. VLSI Logic & Analog Applications

Depletion Load Inverter, Current Sources

Depletion-Load NMOS Inverters: Before complementary CMOS became universal, D-NMOS was heavily utilized as an active pull-up load resistor by tying its gate to its source (VGS = 0V). This configuration provides a constant pull-up current IDSS that charges load capacitance significantly faster than a passive resistor without sacrificing logic swing.

Constant Current Reference

With gate tied to source, D-NMOS behaves as a two-terminal constant current sink: I = IDSS (1 + λ VDS), operating down to VDS = |VTH|.

Power Management Startup

Because D-NMOS is normally ON at 0V, modern Power Management ICs (PMICs) use D-NMOS transistors as high-voltage startup switches to prime internal regulators before CMOS control circuits power up.
