PN Junction Diode Physics & Breakdown Mechanisms

Depletion region electrostatics, built-in barrier potential, Shockley forward conduction, and quantum Zener vs. Avalanche breakdown.

P-Type (Boron) · N-Type (Phosphorus) · Abrupt Junction

1. Metallurgical Junction Formation & Space-Charge Neutrality

NA = 10¹⁵–10¹⁹ cm⁻³, ND = 10¹⁵–10¹⁹ cm⁻³

When a P-type semiconductor crystal containing trivalent acceptor atoms (Boron, NA) is brought into metallurgical contact with an N-type crystal containing pentavalent donor atoms (Phosphorus or Arsenic, ND), steep concentration gradients drive carrier diffusion. Mobile holes diffuse from the P-side into the N-side, while mobile electrons diffuse from the N-side into the P-side.

1. Carrier Recombination

Diffusing majority electrons and holes recombine near the metallurgical boundary (x = 0), annihilating mobile charge carriers.

2. Uncompensated Fixed Cores

Recombination uncovers uncompensated negatively charged Boron acceptor anions (NA⁻) in the P-side and positively charged Phosphorus donor cations (ND⁺) in the N-side.

3. Space-Charge Neutrality

Total negative space charge on the P-side must exactly equal total positive space charge on the N-side: q · NA · xp = q · ND · xn.

Global Charge Neutrality: q · NA · xp = q · ND · xn  ⟹  xp / xn = ND / NA
The space-charge depletion layer extends significantly further into the more lightly doped semiconductor side.

2. Built-In Potential (Vbi) and Total Depletion Width (W)

Poisson Equation Formulation

The fixed ionized cores establish an internal electric field pointing from the positive donors (N-side) to the negative acceptors (P-side). This field opposes further majority carrier diffusion. At thermal equilibrium, carrier diffusion is balanced by field drift, establishing the built-in potential barrier Vbi:

Vbi = (k·T / q) · ln[(NA · ND) / ni²]  ≈  0.65 – 0.95 V
Thermal voltage: Vt = kT/q = 25.85 mV at 300K  ·  Intrinsic concentration: ni ≈ 1.0×10¹⁰ cm⁻³ in Silicon at 300K

Solving Poisson's equation d²V/dx² = -ρ(x)/εs across the step space-charge profile yields the total depletion width W = xp + xn under applied voltage VA:

W = √[ (2·εs / q) · (1/NA + 1/ND) · (Vbi - VA) ]
One-sided extensions: xp = W · ND / (NA + ND)  ·  xn = W · NA / (NA + ND)  ·  εs = 11.7·ε₀

3. Forward Bias vs. Reverse Bias Electrostatics

Barrier Modulation
Forward Bias (VA > 0)

Positive voltage applied to the Anode (P) lowers the electrostatic barrier from Vbi to (Vbi - VA). The depletion width W contracts. Majority holes in the P-region and electrons in the N-region easily overcome the reduced barrier, diffusing exponentially into the opposing neutral regions as injected minority carriers.

Reverse Bias (-VBR < VA < 0)

Negative voltage applied to the Anode widens the barrier to (Vbi + |VA|). The depletion width W expands, pulling majority carriers further away from the metallurgical junction. Majority conduction is quenched, and only a minute reverse saturation leakage current Is (generated by thermal minority electron-hole pairs) drifts across the barrier.

4. The Shockley Diode Equation & Series Resistance

Exponential Conduction Formulation

The ideal current-voltage relationship of a PN junction diode is governed by the Shockley diode equation:

I = Is(T) · [ exp(q·VA / (n·k·T)) - 1 ]
Is(T) = q·A · [ (Dp·pn0 / Lp) + (Dn·np0 / Ln) ] ∝ T³ · exp(-Eg / kT)  ·  Ideality factor n ≈ 1.0–1.1

High Current Series Resistance (Rs): At high forward biases (VA > 0.7 V), substantial current produces an ohmic voltage drop across the quasi-neutral bulk silicon and metal contact interfaces: Vjunction = VA - I · Rs. This transitions the exponential I-V curve into a linear series-resistance slope, preventing non-physical infinite current.

5. Reverse Breakdown: Zener Tunneling vs. Avalanche Multiplication

Sze Empirical Formulation & Tempco

When reverse bias |VA| reaches the critical breakdown voltage VBR, the junction enters reverse breakdown, conducting massive current with minimal increase in voltage. Two distinct quantum-physical mechanisms govern this transition:

6. Electric Field Profile |Emax| & Junction Capacitance (Cj)

Poisson Field Integration
Peak Electric Field

The electric field reaches its maximum magnitude |Emax| precisely at the metallurgical junction (x = 0):

|Emax| = (q · NA · xp) / εs = 2 · (Vbi - VA) / W
Junction Depletion Capacitance

Modulation of space charge under small-signal ac voltage produces depletion capacitance Cj = dQ/dVA:

Cj = εs · A / W = Cj0 / √(1 - VA / Vbi)
