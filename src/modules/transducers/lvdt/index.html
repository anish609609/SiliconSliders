LVDT Theory & Mutual Inductance Physics

PASSIVE INDUCTIVE TRANSDUCER

The Linear Variable Differential Transformer (LVDT) converts rectilinear mechanical displacement x into a proportional, phase-sensitive AC electrical output via differential mutual inductance across three coaxial windings and a movable ferromagnetic Ni-Fe core.

0. The Origin Story: From Patent 2,196,809 to Precision Aerospace

1936 – 1946 · ORIGIN

Before World War II, precision displacement measurement relied heavily on sliding-wiper potentiometers and fragile optical gauges. Mechanical friction, contact bounce, and wiper wear made them fail rapidly inside aircraft engines, hydraulic actuators, and high-vibration industrial machinery.

1. The 1936 Invention (George B. Hoadley)

In 1936, electrical engineer George B. Hoadley conceived and patented the differential transformer displacement transducer (US Patent 2,196,809), demonstrating that opposing secondary windings around a movable ferromagnetic core yield a linear, phase-reversing AC output.

2. Aerospace Commercialization (Herman Schaevitz)

During the 1940s, Herman Schaevitz refined the geometry, magnetic shielding, and winding symmetry to create the ruggedized modern LVDT (founding Schaevitz Engineering in 1946) for wartime flight-test instrumentation and servo actuators.

3. Contactless Longevity

Because the Ni-Fe core floats inside the insulating bobbin without rubbing against any electrical contacts, the LVDT delivered virtually infinite mechanical life, sub-micron resolution, and full galvanic isolation—becoming the gold standard from Apollo valves to modern CNC metrology.

Why It Matters: By uniting differential magnetic coupling with friction-free motion, Hoadley and Schaevitz solved the classic trade-off between mechanical durability and micron-level measurement precision.

1. Series-Opposition Differential Mutual Inductance

Vout = VS1 − VS2

When the center Primary Coil (P) is excited by a sinusoidal carrier vin(t) = √2 Vp sin(2πft), alternating magnetic flux links two identical, symmetrically placed Secondary Coils (S1 and S2). Because S1 and S2 are wired in series opposition, their induced EMFs subtract:

Vout(x) = VS1(x) − VS2(x) = (M1(x) − M2(x)) · ω · Ip ≈ −S · Vp · xeff(x)

Within the rated ±5.0 mm linear stroke, differential mutual inductance (M1 − M2) varies linearly with core displacement x. Beyond ±5.0 mm, magnetic flux fringing at the bobbin ends causes smooth tanh saturation roll-off.

2. The Three Core-Position Regimes

0° ↔ 180° PHASE FLIP

Left of Null (x < 0)

Core couples more flux into S1 (M1 > M2), so VS1 > VS2. Output amplitude grows linearly with |x| and remains in-phase (φ = 0°) with primary excitation Vin.

Magnetic Null (x = 0)

Core sits symmetrically between S1 and S2 (M1 = M2). Equal and opposite secondary EMFs cancel cleanly: Vout = VS1 − VS2 ≈ 0 V.

Right of Null (x > 0)

Core couples more flux into S2 (M2 > M1), so VS2 > VS1. Output polarity reverses sign, creating an instantaneous 180° phase flip relative to Vin.

3. Residual Null Voltage & Phase-Sensitive Demodulation

QUADRATURE & SYNC RECTIFIER

In practical transducers, inter-winding stray capacitance and odd-harmonic magnetic non-linearity prevent exact zero output at x = 0, leaving a small quadrature residual null voltage (90° out of phase with Vin, typically < 1% FS). Because raw RMS voltmeters lose displacement sign across null (yielding a V-shaped curve), a Phase-Sensitive Demodulator (PSD) multiplies vout(t) by a phase-aligned carrier reference and low-pass filters the product to output signed DC voltage proportional to x.

Industrial Signal Conditioning & Field Packaging

SIGNAL CONDITIONING & INSTRUMENTATION

Translating raw LVDT millivolt AC signals into noise-immune industrial outputs (4–20 mA, ±10 VDC) requires monolithic synchronous demodulators, phase-lag compensation, and hermetically sealed magnetic shielding.

AD698 / AD598 Synchronous Demodulators

PHASE-SENSITIVE

Industry-standard ICs such as the Analog Devices AD698 integrate a low-distortion sine-wave oscillator (20 Hz–20 kHz) and two synchronous demodulators. By computing the ratiometric quotient (VA − VB) / (VA + VB) or Vout / Vin, they reject cable resistance drift, temperature variations, and primary amplitude fluctuations, outputting a bipolar DC signal that tracks both magnitude and direction.

Lead / Lag Phase Compensation

PHASE COMPENSATION

Primary winding resistance (Rp) and inductance (Lp) introduce a carrier phase shift that varies with excitation frequency and cable capacitance. An RC lead-lag network aligns the demodulator reference clock exactly with the secondary output phasor, maximizing synchronous gain (cos Δφ → 1) and rejecting 90° quadrature residual null voltage.

IP67 / IP68 Hermetic Packaging & Harsh-Environment Design

316L SS & MU-METAL

Because the Ni-Fe core never physically contacts the bobbin walls, LVDTs exhibit virtually infinite mechanical life and sub-micron resolution. Industrial units laser-weld the coils inside 316L stainless steel or Inconel housings (IP67/IP68), pot windings in high-thermal-conductivity epoxy or ceramic (−55 °C to +220 °C), and line the bore with mu-metal to block nearby 50/60 Hz power-line EMI.
